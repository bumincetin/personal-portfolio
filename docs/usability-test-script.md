# Usability test script

For moderated sessions with people who resemble prospective clients: finance or
operations leads at small and mid-sized companies, and anyone who would be
choosing an external specialist.

**No testing has been run.** This is the script, not a report. Do not cite it as
evidence that the site tests well.

---

## Before you start

Five to seven participants surfaces most of what one round will surface. Recruit
people who have actually chosen a consultant or an agency, not colleagues.

Say this, and mean it:

> I did not design this and I am not being judged by it. I want to find the
> places where it does not work. Please think out loud, and if you get stuck,
> stay stuck for a moment before I help — the stuck moment is the useful part.

Never demonstrate first. Never say "just click the…". If a participant asks what
something does, ask what they expect it to do.

Start each session on `/en` (or `/tr` / `/it`) with no prior explanation.

---

## Task 1 — What is this?

> Look at this page for as long as you need. Then tell me, in your own words,
> what this person does and who they do it for.

**Watching for:** whether they interact with the shelf at all, or scroll past
it. Whether they read a spine. Whether the "expensive problem" framing lands, or
reads as vague.

**Passes if:** within 60 seconds they can name at least two of the four services
unprompted, without using the word "AI" as their whole answer.

**Fails if:** they describe the site as "a portfolio" or "a designer", or cannot
say who it is for.

---

## Task 2 — Is any of this my problem?

> Is there anything here that sounds like a problem you actually have? If so,
> show me where.

**Watching for:** which volume they reach for, and whether they reach it through
the shelf, the navigation, or the written section below the fold.

**Passes if:** they land on a volume that matches the problem they described.

**Fails if:** they cannot map their own situation onto anything, or pick a volume
whose content then turns out to be about something else.

---

## Task 3 — What would I actually get?

> Suppose you hired this person for that. What would you receive?

**Watching for:** whether they find the "What you receive" page, and whether the
book metaphor helps or obstructs — do they turn pages, or try to scroll?

**Passes if:** they can describe a concrete deliverable, not "a report".

**Fails if:** they cannot find it, or they give up turning pages.

---

## Task 4 — Do I believe it?

> Find something on this site that gives you a reason to trust this person's
> work. Then tell me how convincing you find it.

**Watching for:** whether they reach an evidence volume. Whether the limitations
read as honest or as hedging. Watch carefully for the moment they read "it
misses most of them" — does that build confidence or destroy it?

**Passes if:** they find the greenwashing volume and can say both what it
achieved and where it falls short.

**Fails if:** they read the limitations as a reason to leave, or cannot find
evidence at all.

---

## Task 5 — What are the limits?

> Is there anything this person says they will *not* do?

**Watching for:** whether the scope boundaries and the regulated-work notice in
Volume IV are discoverable, or buried too deep in a book.

**Passes if:** they can name at least one thing that is out of scope.

---

## Task 6 — Change the language

> Read this in another language you speak, if you speak one of these.

**Watching for:** whether the switcher is findable at all — it is small, in the
bar and in the mobile sheet. Whether they land on the equivalent page or lose
their place.

**Passes if:** they switch and stay on the same volume.

---

## Task 7 — Get in touch

> You have decided to make contact. Do that, but do not send anything.

**Watching for:** whether they use the shelf's action, the navigation, or the
footer. Whether the disabled-form state reads as broken or as honest. Whether
they notice there is no booking calendar, and whether that bothers them.

**Passes if:** they reach `/contact` and can say what would happen next.

**Fails if:** they read the disabled form as a bug, or expect an instant calendar
booking.

---

## Closing questions

1. Who do you think this site is for?
2. Was there anything you did not believe?
3. Was there anything you wanted to know that you could not find?
4. The site is built as a shelf of books. Did that help you, get in your way, or
   neither?
5. If you had this problem next month, would you write to this person? If not,
   what stopped you?

---

## Recording results

One row per participant per task: pass / partial / fail, time to first correct
action, and a verbatim quote. Fix anything two or more participants fail.

**Do not report a conversion rate from this.** It is a moderated usability test
with a handful of people; it cannot measure conversion, and whether an inquiry
was a *qualified* lead is a judgement made after a conversation, never inferred
from a click.
